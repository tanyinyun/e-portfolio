From Data to Insights: My Marketing Research Journey

01 / GIBBS’ CYCLE

Description

My marketing research project examined the influence of livestream influencers on Gen Z online purchase intention. It considered trust, interactivity, entertainment and expertise as explanatory variables. The project used survey responses from 150 participants and involved data cleaning, reliability testing, t-tests, ANOVA, correlation and regression analysis in SPSS. This gave me an opportunity to connect concepts from marketing research with a practical dataset. Instead of only reading about methods, I had to consider how the information collected could support an interpretation of consumer behaviour.

02 / GIBBS’ CYCLE

Feelings

This section is a draft for me to personalise, because my exact feelings should come from my own recollection. Looking back, an important question is how confident I felt at different stages: designing the study, working with the data and explaining the output. I would like to describe a real moment when my understanding changed, rather than simply saying that the project was challenging. Identifying that moment will help me explain what I learned honestly and show how my confidence developed through practice.

03 / GIBBS’ CYCLE

Evaluation

A useful part of the project was the opportunity to work through several stages of quantitative research. Data preparation and analysis were connected tasks, rather than separate classroom topics. The sample of 150 respondents provided material for practical statistical work. However, having a dataset does not automatically mean that every conclusion is strong. The way participants were recruited, the quality of responses and the wording of questions all need consideration. My evaluation should therefore discuss both what the project enabled me to do and what its evidence could not establish.

04 / GIBBS’ CYCLE

Analysis

The experience helped me recognise the difference between obtaining statistical output and understanding its meaning. A relationship in survey data needs to be interpreted within the research design; it should not automatically be presented as proof that one factor causes another. I also learned why cleaning and reliability checks belong before interpretation. In marketing, a persuasive conclusion must still be supported by appropriate evidence. This connects with my interest in analytics: the goal is not to produce more numbers, but to help someone make a better-informed decision. Clear explanation is therefore part of analytical skill.

05 / GIBBS’ CYCLE

Conclusion

My main learning is that research requires both technical care and communication. SPSS is a useful tool, but software cannot decide whether a research question is meaningful or whether a conclusion is proportionate to the evidence. I want my future work to show a transparent path from the question to the method, the findings and the limitations. This project provides a foundation for my interest in marketing analytics, while also showing that I need further practice in presenting findings simply and linking them to realistic decisions.

06 / GIBBS’ CYCLE

Action Plan

For future research, I would create a written data-cleaning checklist and keep a record of any changes made to the dataset. I would define the analysis plan early, check that each method answers a specific question and explain the sample limitations alongside the results. I would also practise presenting one finding in a short paragraph and one clear chart. Before using this reflection for assessment, I will add a genuine example of a difficulty I encountered, explain how I responded and check that each statement matches my report and personal contribution.
